# Fortune variants — tested against the judge's rubric

The judge (a gardener of thirty years) weighs, most important first:
1. a fortune for a houseplant, about the plant's own future;
2. funny in a way a plant lover would laugh at — a small joke that lands, not a pun groaned at;
3. kind and warm, no doom, no withering, no mockery;
4. sounds like a fortune (prediction cadence, not a slogan, caption or care instruction);
5. one line, at most 120 characters, reads cleanly aloud, no padding.

A fortune that could be said to any plant in any room is weaker than one
that seems written for this plant in this window.

## Shortlist (all 1–120 chars)

| # | Chars | Line | Verdict |
|---|-------|------|---------|
| 1 | 106 | The cat will spare you this year, the sun will stay loyal, and your keeper will finally water you on time. | **CHOSEN** |
| 2 | 107 | Soon a stranger will press their nose to the glass to admire you, and your keeper will take all the credit. | Runner-up |
| 3 | 100 | You will outgrow this pot by spring, and your keeper will blame the sunshine, never the compliments. | Good joke, familiar trope |
| 4 | 99 | Your next leaf arrives on a Tuesday, and your keeper will tell the whole street it was their doing. | Good joke, less warm |
| 5 | 107 | By spring you will need a bigger pot, a taller window, and a keeper brave enough to admit they talk to you. | Good joke, wordier |
| 6 | 98 | A new leaf is coming, and with it a neighbour who presses their nose to the glass just to see you. | Sweet, weak joke |
| 7 | 89 | Your keeper will learn your name this spring, and you will grow an inch just to show off. | Mild joke |

## Why #1 won

- **Specific to this window:** three concrete windowsill realities — the cat
  that stalks the pot, the sun through the glass, the keeper's watering —
  instead of a generic "grow tall and bloom" line any plant could get.
- **Joke that lands:** "the cat will spare you" is a real hazard every
  plant lover recognises; "finally water you on time" is gentle teasing of
  the keeper, not mockery of the plant.
- **Fortune cadence:** three "will" predictions in one breath, the classic
  shape of a fortune-teller's reading.
- **Kind throughout:** no doom, no withering, no threat — the plant is safe,
  the sun is loyal, the keeper is trying.
- **Clean read:** 106 characters, no padding, reads aloud in one breath.

#2 was the closest rival — the stranger at the glass is very windowsill —
but its joke (keeper vanity) is colder than #1's, and "stranger" is less
warm than the cat, the sun and the keeper together.
